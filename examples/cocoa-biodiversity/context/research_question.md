# Research question

**How does biodiversity affect cocoa yields?**

## Framing

- **Subject side (drivers / variables of interest):** biodiversity in cocoa-growing systems — measured as shade-tree species richness, functional diversity, agroforestry vs monoculture structure, pollinator and predator communities, soil biota, etc.
- **Object side (outcomes):** cocoa yield, productivity, pod count, bean count/weight, pest damage that reduces yield.
- **Question type:** directional — *does biodiversity affect yield, and how?* (positive, negative, or non-linear).

## Why this is non-trivial

The literature is internally contested:

- **Diversity-productivity hypothesis**: more shade trees / more biodiversity → ecological services (pollination, pest control, microclimate) → higher cocoa yield.
- **Land-sparing / intensification hypothesis**: full-sun monocultures → higher per-hectare yield → less land needed → biodiversity preserved off-farm. Implies a *negative* on-farm biodiversity–yield correlation.
- **Hump-shaped / threshold hypothesis**: optimum shade level around 30-50%; yields decline at both extremes.

This worked example is called out in the [accumulation README](../../../accumulation/README.md) as illustrating how `object_code` fragments across closely related papers when the vocabulary is not pre-specified.

## Scope

- **Geographic:** global (cocoa is grown in West Africa, Central/South America, Southeast Asia, Pacific).
- **Temporal:** 2010-present (last 15 years; captures the agroforestry-resurgence literature).
- **Languages:** English only (limitation; West African / South American Portuguese and Spanish literature not covered).
- **Method types:** any (observational, experimental, modelling, review).
- **Open access:** restricted to `is_oa:true` to make PDF acquisition tractable.
